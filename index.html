🔎 What Is abl-dualboot?

abl-dualboot is a small set of tools that lets a handheld gaming device run two operating systems side by side: Android and Linux. Normally, switching between the two means digging through boot menus, plugging in a keyboard, or remembering which button to hold during startup. abl-dualboot replaces all of that with a single tap.

It works with the ROCKNIX bootloader, which many modern handhelds already use. The project gives you three ways to trigger the switch, so you can pick whatever feels most natural on your device: a normal Android app with a Quick Settings tile, a Decky plugin for your Linux gaming session, or a small script inside Linux.

You do not need to know how to program. If you can download a file, open it, and follow a few on-screen prompts, you can use this. Everything here is described in plain language, and the download page opens in your normal web browser.

✨ What You Can Do With It

  • Switch with one tap. Jump from Android to Linux, or back again, without hunting for hidden keys at boot.
  • Use a Quick Settings tile. Pull down the Android shade, tap the tile, and the device restarts into the other system.
  • Control it from your Linux game session. The Decky plugin puts the switch right inside the interface you already use every day.
  • Script it in Linux. Prefer a terminal or an automation rule? The Linux script lets you trigger the switch from anywhere.
  • Keep both systems intact. Your Android apps and your Linux files stay where they are. Only the boot target changes.
  • Built for handhelds. Tested around popular devices such as AYN Odin models, Armada handhelds, and other Qualcomm-based retro consoles.

📥 Download and Install on Windows

The download page is where everything starts. Open it in your browser on your Windows PC and save what you need to your computer first. You will move the files to your handheld afterwards.

⬇️ Get the Download

Visit this link to download the application. Once the page opens, use the download section on that page to save the package to a folder you can find again, such as your Downloads folder.

  1. Open the download page. Use the button above while you are on your Windows computer. The page loads in your usual browser.
  2. Save the package. When your browser asks whether to save or open the file, choose Save. Note the folder it lands in.
  3. Connect your handheld. Plug the device into your PC with a USB cable. It should appear as a removable drive in File Explorer, the same way a USB stick does.
  4. Copy the files across. Drag the downloaded package onto the handheld drive, or into the folder your device uses for add-ons. Keep the original copy on your PC as a backup.
  5. Finish the setup on the device. Unplug the cable and follow the on-screen steps on the handheld itself. Most people complete this in a couple of minutes.
  6. Restart once. A single reboot after the first setup lets the new switcher register properly with the bootloader.

🖱️ How to Use the Switcher, Step by Step

Once everything is in place, everyday use is genuinely a single action. Here is the flow from the Android side.

  1. Open the abl-dualboot app from your Android app drawer, or pull down the notification shade and find the Quick Settings tile.
  2. Tap the switch control. The app shows which system you are currently running and which one you will boot into next.
  3. Confirm the choice. The tool sets the boot target and restarts the handheld.
  4. Wait for the device to come back up. It will now start in the other system, with your games, saves, and settings untouched.
  5. To go back, repeat the same steps from the other side, using the Decky plugin or the Linux script.

Nothing is erased during a switch. The tool only tells the bootloader which system to start next, which is why the round trip is so quick.

🧩 The Three Pieces Explained

Android app and Quick Settings tile. This is the friendly face of the project. It is built with Jetpack Compose, so it feels like a normal modern Android app. The tile is the fastest route: one swipe, one tap, done. It works alongside root managers such as APatch and KernelSU, which many handheld owners already use for system-level tweaks.

Decky plugin. If you spend most of your time in the Linux gaming interface, the plugin adds the switch to the same menu you use to change performance settings and other options. No terminal, no keyboard, no leaving your game library.

A small script handles the switch from inside Linux. You can run it manually when you want, or attach it to your own shortcuts and automation if you like that kind of control. All three routes do exactly the same job, so use whichever one matches the moment.

🖥️ Devices and Setup Notes

abl-dualboot is aimed at handhelds that already run the ROCKNIX bootloader and can boot both Android and a Linux-based system such as SteamOS-style distributions. Devices from the AYN Odin family, Armada handhelds, and a range of Qualcomm-based retro consoles are the typical targets.

  • A handheld that already boots two systems, or that you plan to set up that way.
  • The ROCKNIX bootloader installed and working.
  • A USB cable and a Windows PC for copying files.
  • Enough free space on the device for the app and plugin files, which are small.
  • Root access through APatch or KernelSU if you want the Android side to control boot targets.

If your device is not on the list, it may still work. The safest approach is to back up anything important before you try a new setup, then test a single switch in each direction to confirm it behaves the way you expect.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The device still boots the same system. The boot target may not have been written correctly. Open the app again, confirm the target shown on screen, and let the full restart finish before you touch anything.

The Quick Settings tile is missing. Android sometimes hides new tiles. Open the tile editor from the shade and drag the abl-dualboot tile into your active set.

The Decky plugin does not appear. Restart the Linux session once after installing. Plugins usually show up after a fresh start of the interface.

The handheld is not visible on Windows. Try a different USB port and a different cable. Some cables carry power only and cannot transfer files.

Something feels wrong after a switch. Boot back into the system that worked, and check that the package you copied matches the one on the download page. A partially copied file is the most common cause of odd behaviour.
